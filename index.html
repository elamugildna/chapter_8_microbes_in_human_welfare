Class 12 Chapter 8: Microbes in Human Welfare

1. Microbes in Household Products

Curd & Lactic Acid Bacteria (LAB)

LAB (such as Lactobacillus) grow in milk and convert it into curd. Inoculum (starter) contains millions of LAB. They produce acids that coagulate and partially digest milk proteins. LAB enriches nutritional quality by producing Vitamin B12 and plays a beneficial role in checking disease-causing microbes in our gut.

Dough & Baker's Yeast

The puffed-up appearance of dough for idli and dosa is due to CO2 production by bacteria (Leuconostoc & Streptococcus). Bread dough is leavened using Baker's yeast (Saccharomyces cerevisiae). Anaerobic respiration releases CO2 bubbles that make bread soft and spongy.

Traditional Beverage — Toddy

Toddy is a traditional beverage of parts of southern India made by fermenting the sugary sap collected from palms. Microbes are also used to ferment fish, soybean, and bamboo shoots to make edible delicacy foods.

Cheese Production

Swiss cheese: Large holes ('eyes') are produced due to massive CO2 production by the bacterium Propionibacterium sharmanii.
Roquefort cheese: Ripened by growing a specific fungus, Penicillium roqueforti, which imparts its distinct bluish veins and flavour.

Fermented Beverages

Produced using Brewer's yeast (Saccharomyces cerevisiae). Depending on raw materials and whether distillation is used:
• Without distillation: Wine and Beer (lower alcohol percentage).
• With distillation: Whisky, Brandy, and Rum (higher alcohol percentage).

Antibiotics Discovery

Penicillin: First antibiotic discovered by Alexander Fleming while working on Staphylococci bacteria; mould was Penicillium notatum. Full therapeutic potential established by Ernest Chain and Howard Florey (treated wounded soldiers in WWII, Nobel Prize 1945).

3. Microbes in Sewage Treatment (STP) & BOD Concept

Primary Physical Treatment

Involves physical removal of particles through: (1) Sequential filtration (removes floating debris), and (2) Sedimentation in primary settling tanks (removes grit: soil and small pebbles). All solids that settle form primary sludge, and the supernatant forms the primary effluent.

Secondary / Biological Treatment

  1. Aeration Tank: Primary effluent is constantly agitated mechanically and air is pumped into it. Useful aerobic microbes grow rapidly into flocs (masses of bacteria associated with fungal filaments to form mesh-like structures).
  2. BOD Reduction: Flocs consume the major part of the organic matter in effluent. Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD) is the amount of oxygen required by bacteria to oxidize all organic matter in one litre of water. Higher BOD = higher polluting potential. BOD drops significantly after treatment.
  3. Settling Tank: Effluent passes to settling tank where flocs sediment to form activated sludge. A small part is pumped back to the aeration tank as inoculum.
  4. Anaerobic Sludge Digester: Major part of activated sludge is pumped into anaerobic digesters. Anaerobic bacteria digest bacteria and fungi, releasing biogas (a mixture of CH4, H2S, and CO2). Effluent is released into rivers.
Ganga & Yamuna Action Plans: Initiated by the Ministry of Environment and Forests to build major STPs in cities along rivers so that only biologically treated effluent enters these rivers.

4. Biogas, Biocontrol Agents & Biofertilisers

Biogas Production

Methanogens (e.g. Methanobacterium) grow anaerobically on cellulosic materials in cattle rumen and anaerobic sludge digesters. Biogas is ~50-70% methane (CH4) plus CO2 and traces of H2S.
Biogas plants (10-15 ft deep concrete tank with floating cover) were developed by IARI and KVIC in rural India.

Biocontrol Agents

  • • Ladybird beetle: Controls aphids.
  • • Dragonflies: Control mosquitoes.
  • • Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt): Dried spores sprayed to kill butterfly caterpillars.
  • • Trichoderma: Free-living fungus in root ecosystems; controls fungal root pathogens.
  • • Baculoviruses (Nucleopolyhedrovirus): Species-specific, narrow spectrum insecticidal agents; safe for plants, mammals, birds, fish, and non-target beneficial insects in IPM.

Biofertilisers

  • • Rhizobium: Symbiotic N2 fixation in legume root nodules.
  • • Azotobacter & Azospirillum: Free-living N2 fixers in soil.
  • • Mycorrhiza (Glomus): Fungal association; absorbs phosphorus, provides resistance to root pathogens and drought/salinity tolerance.
  • • Cyanobacteria (Anabaena, Nostoc, Oscillatoria): Autotrophic N2 fixers; major biofertiliser in waterlogged paddy fields.
